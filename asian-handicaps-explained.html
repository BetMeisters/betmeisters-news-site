Asian handicaps explained: half, whole and quarter lines, and what each one pays

Asian handicaps take the draw out of football betting. Here is how every line works, from 0 to −1.25, with the exact payout for each result and how to turn a handicap price into a chance.

FootballBasketballSports betting
By the BetMeisters.news desk17 November 20267 min read

Look at almost any BetMeisters tip or acca and you will see lines like FC United of Manchester +0.75, Poole Town +0 or Cape Verde −1.25. They are Asian handicaps, and they are the most-traded football market in the world. They look confusing, but there are only three kinds of line, and once you know which kind you are looking at, you can work out exactly what any result pays.

What an Asian handicap does

A normal football match has three outcomes: home win, draw, away win. An Asian handicap turns it into a two-way bet by giving one team a start (a plus number) and the other a deficit (a minus number), measured in goals. You add the handicap to your team's score at full time, then compare. If your team is still ahead, you win; if level, your stake usually comes back; if behind, you lose.

Because there are only two sides, a fair Asian handicap is close to a 50/50 proposition, and the bookmaker's margin is usually smaller than on the three-way match result. That is one reason the sharp market, and our own Number Cruncher Meister, use them so much.

One rule to remember first: in football, Asian handicaps are settled on the 90-minute score plus stoppage time. Extra time and penalties do not count.

Kind 1: half-goal lines (−0.5, +0.5, −1.5, +1.5)

These are the simplest, because a half goal can never be cancelled out, so there is no draw and no refund. The bet either wins in full or loses in full.

  • −0.5: your team must win. It is the same bet as a straight win, often at a slightly different price.
  • +0.5: your team must not lose. A win or a draw wins the bet: the same as 'double chance'.
  • −1.5: your team must win by two goals or more.
  • +1.5: your team can lose by one goal and the bet still wins.

Kind 2: whole-goal lines (0, −1, +1, −2)

Whole numbers can land exactly on the handicap. When that happens the bet is a push: your stake comes back and you neither win nor lose.

  • 0 (often written +0 or −0): this is draw no bet. A win wins, a draw returns your stake, a defeat loses. Poole Town +0 in tonight's Acca Meister 8-fold is exactly this.
  • −1: win by two or more and you win; win by exactly one and you get your stake back; a draw or defeat loses.
  • +1: a win or a draw wins; lose by exactly one and your stake comes back; lose by two or more and you lose.

Whole lines are popular with cautious bettors because the push takes the sting out of the most likely narrow result.

Kind 3: quarter lines (±0.25, ±0.75, ±1.25)

Quarter lines are the ones that confuse people, but there is a simple trick: a quarter line is two bets in one. Your stake is split in half, and each half goes on the two nearest lines either side.

  • −0.25 = half on 0 (draw no bet) + half on −0.5 (to win).
  • +0.25 = half on 0 + half on +0.5 (win or draw).
  • −0.75 = half on −0.5 + half on −1.
  • +0.75 = half on +0.5 + half on +1.
  • −1.25 = half on −1 + half on −1.5.
  • +1.25 = half on +1 + half on +1.5.

Settle each half separately and add them up. That is where the 'half win' and 'half loss' results come from.

The full payout table, for £10 at 2.00

Here is what £10 at evens (2.00) returns on each quarter line, by result. 'Margin' is your team's goals minus the opponent's.

  • −0.25: win = £20 back. Draw = half loses, half returned: £5 back. Defeat = £0.
  • +0.25: win = £20. Draw = half wins, half returned: £15 back. Defeat = £0.
  • −0.75: win by 2+ = £20. Win by exactly 1 = half wins, half returned: £15. Draw or defeat = £0.
  • +0.75: win or draw = £20. Lose by exactly 1 = half loses, half returned: £5. Lose by 2+ = £0.
  • −1.25: win by 2+ = £20. Win by exactly 1 = half returned, half loses: £5. Draw or defeat = £0.
  • +1.25: win, draw = £20. Lose by exactly 1 = half wins, half returned: £15. Lose by 2+ = £0.

At other prices the maths is the same: a half win returns half your stake at the full price plus the other half back; a half loss returns half your stake and loses the rest.

Turning a handicap price into a chance

Because an Asian handicap is two-way, the maths is easy. Take the two prices, convert each to a percentage (100 divided by the decimal price) and remove the margin by scaling them to add up to 100. If one side is 1.95 and the other 1.95, each is 51.3% before the margin and exactly 50% after it. If the sharp market has a side at 56% and a bookmaker offers 2.00 (50%), that bookmaker is pricing it about 12% above fair: that gap is what we call edge.

Quarter lines complicate this slightly, because the half-win and half-loss results mean 'chance of winning' is not the whole story. The Number Cruncher Meister uses the sharp market's own price on the exact line, so the push and half results are already built in.

Asian handicap v European handicap

Some bookmakers also offer a European (three-way) handicap, written like 'Iceland −1 (three-way)'. It keeps the draw as a separate outcome: with Iceland −1, a one-goal Iceland win is the 'handicap draw', and a bet on Iceland loses. No stake comes back. Always check which one you are betting: the same number can mean a push in one market and a loss in the other.

Handicaps in basketball and handball

The same idea works in points. A basketball line such as Zalgiris −9.5 means Zalgiris must win by 10 or more; there is no draw because of the half point. Whole lines push in the usual way: Botevgrad +2 returns the stake if they lose by exactly two. In basketball, overtime counts for handicaps and totals at most UK bookmakers; in handball, check the market, because some are settled at the end of normal time.

Common mistakes

  • Treating −0.75 like −1. On −0.75, a one-goal win still pays half at the full price. On −1, it is only a refund.
  • Forgetting extra time does not count. A cup tie that is 1-1 after 90 minutes is a draw for handicap purposes, whatever happens after.
  • Quarter lines in accas. A half-won leg is not a winner: most bookmakers count it at a reduced price, (1 + price) ÷ 2, so the acca pays less than you expect. A half-lost leg keeps the acca alive at half the price, 0.5. Check your bookmaker's rules.
  • Mixing up the sides. −1.25 for one team is the same bet as +1.25 for the other; make sure the team name and sign match what you meant.

Which line should you choose?

The same match is usually offered on several lines at once, each at a different price. Moving from −0.5 to −0.75 to −1 makes your bet harder to win, so the price rises; moving the other way makes it easier and the price falls. None is better in itself: the question is always whether the price is above the fair chance for that line.

As a rule of thumb, the line closest to 50/50 is where the market is most accurate, because that is where most of the money goes. The further you move from it, the more the bookmaker tends to widen its margin. If you like a favourite but fear a one-goal win, a quarter line such as −0.75 gives you half the payout in that case instead of nothing; if you like an underdog but fear a narrow defeat, +0.75 or +1 cushions it.

One last tip: compare the Asian handicap price with the matching three-way price. A team at −0.5 should pay almost exactly the same as the team to win; if one is clearly bigger, that is the better bet for the same outcome.

The quick reference

  • .5 lines (−0.5, +1.5): win or lose, nothing in between.
  • Whole lines (0, −1, +2): land on the number and your stake comes back.
  • Quarter lines (.25, .75): your stake is split across the two lines either side, so one result per line can pay half.
