Statistics watchdog says Britain's big gambling survey has made "significant progress", with four fixes still to do

The Office for Statistics Regulation has found that six of its nine earlier recommendations on the Gambling Survey for Great Britain have been met. The Gambling Commission promises a further update by February 2027.

Licensing and regulationUKGeneral news
By the BetMeisters.news desk20 November 20264 min read

The survey that underpins much of the official picture of gambling in Great Britain has passed an important check. The Office for Statistics Regulation (OSR), the independent body that oversees official statistics in the UK, has reviewed the Gambling Survey for Great Britain (GSGB) again and found what it calls "significant progress" in strengthening it, the Gambling Commission said on Thursday 19 November.

What the review found

OSR's earlier report on the survey made nine recommendations. The new review concludes that six of them have now been met. Three are still outstanding, and OSR has added one new recommendation, so four items remain open.

The Commission put the progress down to "considerable work" on the survey, and said it will publish a further update on the remaining recommendations by February 2027. Both the Commission's response and OSR's latest review are published on the Commission's website.

What the GSGB is, and why it matters

The GSGB is the Commission's main survey of gambling behaviour in Great Britain. It collects data from around 20,000 people a year, and the Commission describes it as one of the largest consumer surveys of its kind in the world.

Its figures feed into how the regulator, the government and the industry talk about gambling: how many adults gamble, on what, and how many report experiencing harm. That is why its quality matters to everyone, from campaigners to operators. Numbers from the survey are regularly quoted in debates about affordability checks, stake limits and advertising.

The Commission asked OSR to review the survey in 2024, when it was a new source of official statistics, to test it against the Code of Practice for Statistics and to build trust in the results.

What the Commission says

Sarah Fox, the Commission's Executive Director for National Lottery, Research and Statistics, and Policy, called the survey "a robust and authoritative source of evidence for gambling in Great Britain". She also noted a limit that applies to every survey of this kind: it estimates people's self-reported experiences, not activity that has been independently checked.

Why this is worth knowing

Surveys and real betting data can tell different stories, and both sides of the gambling debate use the numbers that suit them. An independent statistics regulator signing off most of its recommendations makes the GSGB harder to dismiss, while the four open items show it is still a work in progress. The next checkpoint is the Commission's update, due by February 2027.

Elsewhere this month, the Commission has also published new estimates on young people and gambling (12 November) and set out plans to go after the money behind illegal gambling sites, not just the sites themselves (9 November).
