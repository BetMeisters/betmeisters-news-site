How to check a gambling licence: a step-by-step guide for UK, Malta, Curaçao and beyond

A logo in the footer proves nothing. Here is how to check, in five minutes, whether a betting or casino site really holds the licence it claims, and what each licence actually protects.

Licensing and regulation
By the BetMeisters.news desk16 November 202610 min read

Every betting and casino site says it is licensed. Some are telling the truth, some hold a licence that does not cover you, and some are simply copying a regulator's logo. The Malta Gaming Authority has publicly named 15 websites that claimed an MGA licence they did not have, and it warned that such sites often show a slightly altered logo with no working link and no licence number.

The good news is that checking takes about five minutes, and you do not need to trust anyone's word, including ours. This guide shows you how to check a licence step by step, what to look for on the most common regulators' registers, and what each licence actually means for your money if something goes wrong.

Why the licence matters

A licence is not a seal of quality. It does not mean the odds are good, the bonuses are fair or the support is fast. It means a regulator has checked who owns the business, requires it to follow rules, and can punish it if it does not. In practice, a good licence gives you four things:

  • Someone to answer to. A licensed operator must follow rules on fair games, advertising, money laundering and protecting vulnerable customers, and the regulator can fine it or take its licence away.
  • A dispute route. Strong regulators require operators to offer free, independent dispute resolution if a complaint about your money is not resolved.
  • Safer-gambling tools. Deposit limits, time-outs and self-exclusion, which in Great Britain must include GAMSTOP.
  • Tested games. Random number generators and game returns are tested by approved labs.

A weak licence, or none, gives you far less of all four. And a licence that does not cover your country gives you very little at all, because you are not the customer the regulator is protecting.

Step 1: find the licence details on the site

Scroll to the footer of the site, or open its terms and conditions. A properly licensed operator will name:

  • the legal company that runs the site, which is often different from the brand name;
  • the regulator;
  • a licence or account number.

If the site shows a regulator's logo but no company name and no licence number, treat that as a warning sign. If the logo is meant to be clickable, as the MGA's is, click it: a genuine MGA seal links to a verification page on the MGA's own website, authorisation.mga.org.mt. Then go to the regulator's website yourself, rather than following a link from the site you are checking, and look it up on the official register.

Step 2: check the UK Gambling Commission register

For anyone gambling in Great Britain, this is the one that matters. The Gambling Commission's public register is free and searchable by business name or account number. When you find the operator, check four things:

  • Status. The licence should be active. A licence can be suspended, revoked or surrendered, and an old entry may still appear.
  • Activities. A remote betting licence covers online sports betting, and a remote casino licence covers online casino games. A sportsbook licence does not cover the casino, and the reverse.
  • Domain names. The register lists the website addresses the licence covers. The site you are using should be on that list. This is the quickest way to catch a fake: a copycat site can claim a real company's licence, but it cannot add itself to the register.
  • Regulatory actions. The register shows fines, settlements and warnings. A large operator with one old action is not unusual, but repeated failures on money laundering or customer protection tell you how the business is run.

Two things often confuse people. First, a Gambling Commission licence covers Great Britain: England, Scotland and Wales. Northern Ireland has its own gambling law. Second, many brands share one licence. A single company can run several sites, and some brands run on a 'white label' basis under another company's licence. That is allowed, but the company on the register is the one legally responsible, and it is worth knowing who it is.

Step 3: check the Malta Gaming Authority register

Malta is the biggest licensing hub for European online casinos. The MGA's Licensee Register is on its website and lists each licensed company, its licence number and the websites it operates. The checks are the same as for the UK: the company name should match the site's footer, the licence should be active, and the website should be listed.

An MGA licence is a serious licence with real supervision, testing and dispute routes. It is not a licence to serve every country, though. An MGA-licensed operator taking bets from Great Britain also needs a UK Gambling Commission licence, and many European countries now require their own national licence. If a site is MGA-licensed but you live in a country with its own licensing system, check whether the operator holds that country's licence too.

Step 4: Curaçao and the new LOK regime

For years Curaçao was the cheapest and loosest place to get a gambling licence. A handful of 'master licence' holders issued sublicences to thousands of sites, and players had very little protection if something went wrong. That system has been replaced.

Curaçao's new gambling law, the National Ordinance on Games of Chance (known by its Dutch initials, LOK), came into force on 24 December 2024. The old Gaming Control Board became the Curaçao Gaming Authority (CGA), an independent public body with wider powers. By December 2025 the CGA said it had granted 604 provisional licences, 551 to operators and 53 to suppliers, and rejected 139 applications. It was also reviewing provisional licences to decide whether they qualify for a full licence, certifying game-testing labs and processing applications from dispute-resolution providers.

What this means for you: a Curaçao licence today should be a direct licence from the CGA, not a sublicence under an old master licence. Check the licence on the CGA's own website, check it is current and not just 'provisional' indefinitely, and check what complaint route the operator offers. A Curaçao licence is still a lighter form of protection than a UK or Malta licence, and many countries regard Curaçao-licensed sites that serve their residents as unlicensed.

Step 5: other regulators you will see

  • Gibraltar (Gambling Commissioner, under the Government of Gibraltar) and the Isle of Man (Gambling Supervision Commission) license several large betting and casino groups. Both keep public lists of licensees.
  • Ireland set up a new regulator, the Gambling Regulatory Authority of Ireland, under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024, replacing the old patchwork of betting and gaming laws.
  • Canada: in Ontario, online operators are registered by the Alcohol and Gaming Commission of Ontario (AGCO) and contracted with iGaming Ontario, and the AGCO publishes the list of registered operators. Other provinces mostly run their own government sites.
  • United States: online sports betting and casino gaming are licensed state by state, for example by the New Jersey Division of Gaming Enforcement. A licence in one state does not cover another. 'Sweepstakes' casinos generally operate without a gambling licence, under sweepstakes law, and a growing number of states are banning them.
  • Kahnawake, Anjouan and other small licensing bodies license many international sites. Their protection is limited, and in many countries these sites are not legal.

A worked example: checking Casumo in five minutes

Here is the check we ran for our own Casumo review, step by step, so you can see how quick it is.

The footer. Normally you start here. Our checking server cannot open gambling sites, so we started from the details published for the brand: Recro Limited, Gambling Commission account 61549. A reader would simply read them off the footer.

The register. Searching the Commission's register for 61549 brings up Recro Limited with an active licence.

The domain. On the domain names tab, casumo.com is listed as an active domain on that account. So the site we were looking at is the one the licence covers, and not a copy.

The record. The regulatory actions tab shows no actions for Recro Limited. Casumo's earlier licence holder, a different company, was fined GBP 6 million in 2021. That is useful history, but it falls outside the three-year window we use for scoring.

The complaints route. The terms should name an independent dispute-resolution provider. This is the one step we could not finish from our checking server, so it is on the list for our human reviewer.

Result: a genuine, active UK licence that covers the site, with a clean recent record. That is why Casumo scores 10 for licence strength and 10 for the domain match. None of it tells you whether the games pay fairly or the terms are reasonable. Those are separate checks.

Red flags that a licence claim is fake or weak

  • A regulator logo with no licence number and no company name.
  • A logo that does not link to the regulator, or links to a page on the casino's own site.
  • The website is not listed on the regulator's register, even though the company is.
  • The company in the footer does not match the company on the register.
  • The site accepts players from your country but holds no licence there, and says nothing about which countries it may serve.
  • Terms that name no dispute-resolution provider.
  • Pressure to deposit in crypto only, or to use a VPN. Using a VPN to reach a site that is not allowed in your country breaks the site's own terms and can be used to refuse your winnings.

FAQs

How do I know if a betting site is licensed in the UK? Search for the operator's company name or account number on the Gambling Commission's public register, check the licence is active, and check the site's web address is listed under its domain names.

Is an MGA licence valid in the UK? No. A site serving customers in Great Britain needs a Gambling Commission licence, whatever other licences it holds.

Is a Curaçao licence safe? Curaçao's new regulator, the CGA, has tightened the rules since the LOK took effect on 24 December 2024, but its protection is still lighter than the UK's or Malta's. Many countries treat Curaçao-licensed sites as unlicensed for their residents.

What if a site has no licence at all? Do not use it. You will have no regulator to complain to and no guaranteed dispute route if it refuses to pay.

Does a licence mean the odds or games are fair? A licence means the games are tested and the operator follows rules. It does not mean the prices are good. That is what our price and RTP checks are for.
