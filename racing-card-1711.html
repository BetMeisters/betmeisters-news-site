Tuesday's racing: Skelton's Carlisle raid, Southwell under lights and a big-field Limerick card

Three meetings and 25 races. Dan Skelton sends six to Carlisle, with Bossman Jack and Turbocharged odds-on. Southwell races into the evening and Limerick has five 17-runner fields. Every race, with the early market and the angles that matter.

Tuesday's racing in Britain and Ireland is spread across three meetings: Carlisle over jumps in the early afternoon, Limerick over hurdles at the same time, and Southwell on the all-weather from 4.30pm into the evening. That is 25 races in all, from 11.50am to 8.30pm UK time (2.50pm to 11.30pm in Kampala).

The prices below are early prices, taken on Monday evening from the main UK and Irish bookmakers. At this stage only a handful of firms price each race, so the market is thin and will move a lot by the morning. We show the favourite and the best early price, and we explain where the form and the trainers stand. We add fair chances (the market with the bookmakers' margin taken out) once at least six bookmaker groups price a race, which usually happens by mid-morning. Our figures come from The Racing API racecards and its bookmaker feed.

Updated 07:35 Kampala time, Tuesday. The market has filled out overnight: 16 to 19 bookmaker groups now price most races (Carlisle's 1.25 still has only two). The main moves: at Carlisle, Doubly Blessed (12.50) has been backed from about 5/4 into 4/5, a fair chance of about 50%; Bossman Jack (12.20) is about 8/13, a fair chance of 58%, with Kaka's Cousin now second favourite at 5/1; Coconut Man is joint favourite with They're Chancers at 3/1 in the 11.50; Doyen du Bar is now a clear 2/1 favourite in the 2.25 (27%); and in the 3.30 Air Apparent (about 15/8) has edged ahead of Mr Lincoln (2/1). At Limerick, Detroit Maverick (12.40) is about 10/11, a fair chance of 44%. At Southwell, Hayate in the 8.00 is the evening's shortest favourite at about 8/15 (60%). Several Limerick races list more declared horses than the field size, so some will not run: check the final field before you bet.

Carlisle: the Skelton show

Carlisle stages eight races over hurdles and fences. The story of the day is Dan Skelton. His yard is in red-hot form: 19 winners from 78 runners in the last 14 days, a strike rate of about 24%, the best of any trainer at the three meetings. He sends runners to six of the eight races, with Harry Skelton riding most of them, and three of them are favourites in the early betting.

11.50 · Maiden hurdle, 2m3f (15 runners, Class 4)

A big field of maidens opens the card. They're Chancers (Nicky Richards, Danny McMenamin) is the early favourite at up to 3/1. His form figures read 1/2 and he has not been seen for 323 days, so fitness is a question. Coconut Man (Skelton) is next at around 9/2, and Lucinda Russell and Michael Scudamore's Ar Ais Aris, second last time out 33 days ago, is a similar price. Russell and Scudamore are also in good form, with 9 winners from 37 runners in 14 days. With 15 runners, many of them unexposed, this is a race for small stakes at most.

12.20 · Novices' chase, 2m3f (6 runners, Class 3)

The best race on the card on official ratings. Bossman Jack is rated 148, 14lb clear of the rest of the field, and is odds-on at around 4/6. His last-season form figures read 31161. The catch is the usual one with novice chasers: jumping fences at speed is a new skill, and a horse that falls or makes a bad mistake loses regardless of his rating. He has also been off for 220 days. His stablemate A Pai de Nom (rated 134) is second favourite at around 13/2, with Kalkbrenner (Ben Pauling, 134) at 7/1. At 4/6 the market makes Bossman Jack a strong favourite, but not a certainty.

12.50 and 1.25 · Novices' hurdles, 2m1f (two divisions)

The race is split into two divisions, and Skelton has the favourite in both. In the first, Doubly Blessed (form 12) is around 5/4, with Donald McCain's Throatlash, rated 117, who won three in a row before a fall, next at 9/2. In the second, Turbocharged is the shortest-priced runner of the day at around 4/9. He won the second of his two starts last season. The main danger on ratings is Bohemond Antioch (McCain, rated 113) at around 6/1.

Both divisions are qualifiers for the 'Go North' Night Nurse series, so local trainers have a reason to run their better young horses here. Odds-on novices are often the most reliable favourites in jump racing, but 4/9 still means the market expects him to get beaten roughly three times in ten.

1.55 · Handicap hurdle, 3m1f (12 runners, Class 5)

The marathon of the card, and a genuinely open race. Max Comley's King Berry is favourite at around 7/2, with Coventry (Lisa Harrison) next. Coventry has won two of his last three races, form 171, and ran 17 days ago. Half Track and Ebselysees, the top-weight on 100, are both around 9/2 to 8/1. Over three miles in November at Carlisle, stamina will count.

2.25 · Handicap hurdle, 2m1f (17 runners, Class 4)

A full field. Skelton's Razors Edge (rated 113, off for 323 days) and Nicky Richards' 10-year-old Doyen du Bar (rated 122, last-time-out winner) share favouritism at around 3/1. The top-rated pair, Doyen du Bar and Mick Collins (122), carry the most weight. In a 17-runner handicap the favourite wins far less often than in a novice race, so the each-way terms matter as much as the win price.

3.00 · Handicap hurdle, 2m1f (17 runners, Class 5)

The most open race of the day: no horse is shorter than about 6/1 in the early betting. Skelton's Holeheartedly heads the market despite form of 6088-7, which suggests the market expects a big step forward. Rebecca Menzies' Bruce Banner (second eight days ago) and Raffles Wonder are next, both around 13/2.

3.30 · Conditional jockeys' handicap chase, 2m (6 runners, Class 5)

The card closes with a small-field chase for conditional riders. Iain Jardine's Mr Lincoln won last time and is favourite at around 7/4. Seamus Mullins' Air Apparent, also a last-time-out winner and top-rated on 105, is around 11/4.

Limerick: big fields, yielding ground

Limerick's eight races are all over hurdles or in a bumper, on yielding ground. Five of them have 17 runners, so expect big prices and generous each-way terms. Gordon Elliott is the trainer to note: 12 winners from 73 runners in 14 days.

12.10 and 12.40 · Maiden hurdles

In the 12.10 (2m2½f), Poet's Duke (Paul Nolan) is favourite at around 7/4 despite form of 75-07, with Con Did It and Elliott's Western Note next. In the 12.40, an opportunity race for less experienced riders, Elliott's Detroit Maverick is the day's strongest Irish favourite at around 8/11. He has been placed in four of his last five starts without winning, so he is consistent rather than proven.

1.10 and 1.40 · Handicap hurdles, 2m

The 1.10 is a mares' handicap. Cathryns Ruby, a last-time-out winner and top-rated on 108, is around 5/2, with Jumbos Jet (3/1) and Galileo Springs next. In the 1.40, The Monkey Pole runs again just five days after his last race and is favourite at around 7/4.

2.10, 2.40 and 3.15 · Staying handicap hurdles

Three 17-runner handicaps that will decide many accumulators. The two divisions over 2m5f have no runner shorter than 7/2. The Cobblers Wife (Anthony Mullins) and Ardera Gun head the first. Kayla's Friend and Elliott's Jonzac head the second. In the 3m 3.15, Emmet Mullins' Cock And Bull is favourite at around 7/4. Mullins has two winners from five runners in the last 14 days.

3.50 · Ladies' pro/am bumper, 2m

A flat race for jumps-bred horses, ridden by amateur and professional women riders. Gavin Cromwell's Marble Hill (form 2-3) and Elliott's Todo Bien are close in the betting at around 7/4 and 9/4.

Southwell: evening all-weather

Southwell's Tapeta track takes over from 4.30pm with nine races. The going is standard, and every race is a rider-restricted race, so many of the jockeys claim weight. All-weather evening cards tend to have well-handicapped, recently raced horses, and the market is usually accurate.

4.30 and 5.00 · Sprints over 4½ furlongs

The 4.30 is a 14-runner handicap over Southwell's extended five furlongs. Geoff Oldroyd's Managing Director is favourite at around 4/1, and nobody else is shorter than 13/2. The 5.00 is a five-runner nursery for two-year-olds. Richard Hannon's Urumita won last time and is around 2/1. Undercover Affair, also a recent winner, is top-rated on 82.

5.30 and 6.00 · Middle-distance races, 1m4f

In the 5.30 handicap, Andrew Balding's Fahidi (won last time, claiming apprentice) is favourite at around 7/4. The 6.00 is a fillies' novice race. Maneater (Richard Spencer), second last time out, is around 3/1, but the Crisford-trained Danseuse and Harry Charlton's Bintnaseefa are close behind. Novices with only one or two runs can improve a lot, so this one is less predictable than its small field suggests.

6.30 to 8.30 · The evening handicaps and a novice

At 6.30, Michael Herrington's Anthropologist has won his last two races and is around 3/1. Karl Burke's Moving Shadow is next. The two 7f divisions at 7.00 and 7.30 are open, except that Edward Bethell's Edmond Halley, a dual last-time-out winner, is around 6/4 in the second. The 8.00 novice has the best two-year-olds of the evening. Roger Varian's Hayate, already rated 90 after winning last time, is around 11/10, with William Haggas' unbeaten Miss Deeds (won on her only start) at around 7/4. The card closes with a 14-runner sprint handicap headed by Methgal at around 3/1.

What the early market says

Four horses are odds-on or close to it: Turbocharged (4/9), Bossman Jack (4/6), Detroit Maverick (8/11) and Hayate (11/10). Three of them are novices returning from a break or stepping up in class, which is where shocks happen most often. Odds-on does not mean certain. A price of 4/9 implies about 69%, which still means the favourite gets beaten roughly one time in three.

The value, if there is any, is usually in the big handicaps, where bookmakers disagree most. On Monday evening the early prices for the same horse differed by up to two or three points between firms in the 17-runner races. That is why comparing prices matters more than any tip.
