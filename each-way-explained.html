Each-way betting explained, with the maths

An each-way bet is two bets in one. Here is how places, fractions and payouts actually work.

Horse racingSports betting
By the BetMeisters.news desk16 November 20264 min read

An each-way bet is two equal bets: one on the horse to win, and one on it to place (finish in the top few). A £5 each-way bet costs £10.

Places and fractions

The bookmaker sets how many places pay and at what fraction of the win odds. Common UK terms are 1/4 or 1/5 of the odds for the first 2, 3 or 4 places, depending on the number of runners and the type of race. Some bookmakers offer extra places on big races.

Worked example

£5 each-way at 9.00 (8/1), 1/5 odds for 3 places:

  • Wins: win part £5 × 9.00 = £45; place part pays at 1 + (8 × 1/5) = 2.60, so £13. Total back £58 from £10 staked.
  • Finishes 2nd or 3rd: win part loses; place part returns £13. Total back £13 from £10.
  • Unplaced: both parts lose. −£10.

When each-way makes sense

Each-way is best value when the place part is generous: bigger fields where the terms pay more places than the race really deserves, or when a bookmaker adds an extra place. It is usually poor value on short-priced favourites, where the place part returns very little.

Each-way doubles your stake. Decide your total stake first, then split it.
