From Wembley winners to the Kassam dugout: the remarkable, very human story of Aaron Ramsey's Oxford United

The man who won FA Cups for Arsenal, earned more than any British player before him at Juventus, lost a dog and a contract in Mexico and was relegated as a caretaker by an Oxford free kick now has Oxford one defeat from 13 league games and in the promotion race. Today it is Wycombe at home.

FootballSports bettingUK
By the BetMeisters.news desk21 November 202617 min read

Five months ago, Oxford United's choice of head coach looked like one of the bigger gambles of the summer. The club had just been relegated from the Championship, finishing 22nd and four points from safety. The man they turned to had retired as a player only in April, had never held a permanent managerial job, and had three games of caretaker experience, none of them won.

Today, Saturday 21 November, Aaron Ramsey takes Oxford into a home game against Wycombe Wanderers with his side fourth in League One, unbeaten in ten league matches, with one league defeat all season and the second-best points-per-game record in the division. They have played fewer games than every side above them.

This is the story of how he got here: a career that went from a record-breaking teenage debut in Cardiff to three FA Cup winners' medals at Arsenal, the richest contract ever given to a British player, a European semi-final with Wales, a missing beagle in central Mexico and, finally, a dugout in Oxford. Then we look at today's game, the numbers behind Oxford's start and what the market thinks of their promotion chances.

Facts, results and quotes below come from the sources listed at the foot of the page. Match chances are from Pinnacle's prices with the bookmaker margin removed (the sharp market), taken on Saturday morning UK time. Times are UK.

The boy from Caerphilly who broke John Toshack's record

Aaron James Ramsey was born in Caerphilly on 26 December 1990 and joined Cardiff City's academy as an eight-year-old schoolboy. On 28 April 2007 he came off the bench in a 1-0 defeat to Hull City and became Cardiff's youngest-ever first-team player at 16 years and 124 days, taking a record that had belonged to John Toshack, the man who later managed Wales.

The following season he was in the Cardiff side that reached the 2008 FA Cup final, still only 17. It was the first sign of a theme that runs through his whole career: Ramsey and the FA Cup. Cardiff lost that day to Portsmouth, but the competition would give him the two most famous goals he ever scored.

By then the big clubs were circling. Ramsey has since explained publicly why he turned down Manchester United to join Arsenal, then managed by Arsène Wenger. On 13 June 2008 he signed for Arsenal for a fee of around £5 million. Five months later, on 19 November 2008, he won his first Wales cap, aged 17, in a 1-0 win in Denmark.

Stoke, 27 February 2010

Every long Ramsey story has to stop here. On 27 February 2010, aged 19 and establishing himself in Arsenal's midfield, he suffered a broken tibia and fibula in a tackle by Ryan Shawcross at Stoke City. It was the kind of injury that ends careers or, at the very least, changes them for good.

The way back was slow and unglamorous. There were loan spells at Nottingham Forest (five appearances in late 2010, hit by bad weather and limited game time) and back at Cardiff early in 2011 (six appearances, one goal), simply to get minutes into his legs. Plenty of supporters who remember those seasons will tell you that the confidence took longer to return than the fitness. What came next is why the story is worth telling.

Arsenal: 369 games, 64 goals and two FA Cup final winners

The 2013-14 season was the breakthrough. Ramsey scored 16 goals from midfield and was named Arsenal's Player of the Season. Steven Gerrard called him the best attacking midfielder in the Premier League. And on 17 May 2014, in the FA Cup final against Hull City, he scored the winner in the 109th minute to end Arsenal's nine-year wait for a trophy. It is still one of the most replayed goals in the club's modern history.

A year later he played the full 90 minutes as Arsenal beat Aston Villa 4-0 to retain the Cup. On 27 May 2017, he did it again: a late headed winner against Chelsea in a 2-1 final. Two FA Cup final winning goals in four seasons is a record very few players can match.

In all, Ramsey played 369 times for Arsenal and scored 64 goals, won three FA Cups (2014, 2015, 2017) and was named the club's Player of the Season for a second time in 2017-18. He was the definition of a box-to-box midfielder: late runs into the area, work off the ball and a happy knack of turning up in the biggest games. He left in 2019 at the end of his contract, one of the last links to the Wenger era.

Juventus: the best-paid British player there has ever been

The move to Turin was agreed in February 2019 and took effect that July, on a four-year deal. The headline number was eye-catching: a basic salary reported at around £400,000 a week, which made Ramsey the highest-earning British footballer ever on basic pay at the time. For a player whose career had nearly been ended by a broken leg nine years earlier, it was quite a thing.

The trophies came too. Ramsey won Serie A in 2019-20, the Supercoppa Italiana in 2020 and the Coppa Italia in 2020-21. Injuries meant he never quite became the regular he had been in London: 70 appearances and six goals in all competitions. Juventus ended his contract in July 2022.

Before that, he spent the second half of 2021-22 on loan at Rangers. He picked up a Scottish Cup winner's medal (unused in the final) and came off the bench in extra time of the Europa League final in Seville, where his penalty in the shoot-out was saved by Eintracht Frankfurt's Kevin Trapp. Even the low points of Ramsey's career happen on big stages.

Nice, then home to Cardiff

In August 2022 he joined Nice on a free transfer and scored on his Ligue 1 debut, an equaliser in a 1-1 draw with Toulouse. He made 27 league appearances in a season on the Côte d'Azur before leaving by mutual consent in July 2023.

Then he went home. Ramsey re-signed for Cardiff City in summer 2023 on a two-year deal. Injuries limited him to 21 league appearances and three goals across two seasons, but it was during this spell that the next chapter began. While playing, he studied for his coaching qualifications with the Football Association of Wales and coached at the club. In September 2024 the FAW presented him with his UEFA A Licence and Elite Youth A Licence, completed over two years alongside his playing career.

The caretaker job, and the Oxford free kick

This is where the story bends back on itself. On 19 April 2025, with Cardiff in deep relegation trouble in the Championship, the club sacked Omer Riza and put Ramsey, the Wales captain and still recovering from hamstring surgery, in charge for the last three games of the season.

His first game as a manager was on Easter Monday, 21 April 2025, at the Cardiff City Stadium. The opponents were Oxford United. Yousef Salech headed Cardiff in front on 56 minutes, and for half an hour Ramsey's managerial career had a dream start. Then, about 11 minutes from time, Cameron Brannagan stepped up to a free kick from around 25 yards and smashed it in. 1-1. Sky Sports called it one of the most powerful set pieces of the season.

The point that Brannagan's free kick cost Cardiff mattered. Five days later, a 0-0 draw with West Brom confirmed Cardiff's relegation to League One. Ramsey's caretaker record finished at played three, won none, drawn two, lost one. He left the club when his contract ran out on 30 June 2025.

Fast forward 19 months, and Brannagan is one of Ramsey's own midfielders at Oxford, expected to start in the centre of the park today. Football has a sense of humour.

Mexico, Pumas and Halo the beagle

If the Cardiff chapter was painful, the next one was simply strange. In July 2025 Ramsey joined Pumas UNAM in Mexico City on a free transfer. He made his Liga MX debut on 24 August in a 0-0 draw with Puebla and, a week later, scored a 92nd-minute winner against Atlas. For a few weeks it looked like a lovely late-career adventure.

Then, in October 2025, the family's beagle, Halo, went missing in San Miguel de Allende in the state of Guanajuato, near a local dog shelter. The search took over. Ramsey offered a reward of $10,000 (about £7,500) for information leading to her safe return, and explained publicly that the cameras had not been recording, the tracker could not find her and there had been no sightings. His wife, Colleen, said she believed Halo had likely been taken and refused to accept that she had simply disappeared. Ramsey missed matches and training sessions while the search went on.

As far as has been reported, Halo has not been found. On 31 October 2025, after six appearances and one goal, Ramsey's Pumas contract was ended; he later said publicly that the termination came as a surprise to him, and returned to Wales. It is hard to think of another former Premier League and Serie A champion-level player whose career wound down this way. It is also impossible not to feel for the family.

Retirement, and a phone call from Oxford

On 7 April 2026, aged 35, Ramsey announced his retirement as a player. He left with 86 Wales caps and 21 international goals. Wales made him their youngest-ever captain in 2011, at 20 years and 90 days. At Euro 2016 he was one of the players of the tournament as Wales reached the semi-finals: four assists, joint-most at the finals, and a place in UEFA's Team of the Tournament. He also played for Great Britain at the London 2012 Olympics and was part of the Wales squad that reached the 2022 World Cup, the country's first for 64 years.

Meanwhile, Oxford's season had fallen apart. Gary Rowett had been dismissed after a year in charge, and Matt Bloomfield, the Wycombe Wanderers legend appointed on 9 January 2026, could not keep the club up. Oxford finished 22nd. On 20 June 2026 the club confirmed Bloomfield's departure, thanking him for representing the club with professionalism and integrity. Three days later, on 23 June, they named Aaron Ramsey head coach.

It was not the obvious appointment, and it was not the name most supporters expected. Coverage at the time described it as a gamble, and the facts explained why: a relegated club, a first permanent job, three caretaker games behind him and none of them won. Ramsey's first words as Oxford head coach were about culture rather than his own CV. He said it was an honour and privilege to take the job, and that he wanted to bring high standards, professionalism and work ethic to the club.

The numbers that stand out:

  • One league defeat in 13. Oxford are unbeaten in their last ten league games, which means the only loss came in the first three matches of Ramsey's reign.
  • 26 points from 13 games, exactly 2.00 a game. That is just behind leaders Huddersfield (29 from 14 is 2.07) and better than Stockport (1.93) and Sheffield Wednesday (1.73). Over a 46-game season, 2.00 a game is a 92-point pace.
  • Games in hand. Oxford have played one game fewer than Huddersfield and two fewer than Stockport and Wednesday. Win the game in hand on Huddersfield and they would be level on points with the top two.
  • The defence. Eleven goals conceded in 13 games is the second-best defensive record in the division, with three clean sheets in the last four league games.
  • A +11 goal difference (22 scored, 11 conceded), built on control rather than chaos: five of the last six league games had two goals or fewer.

Oxford's last six league results, oldest first: Mansfield 1-1, Wigan won 2-1, Bradford 0-0, Stockport won 1-0, Barnsley 1-1, Reading won 2-0. The Reading win, with goals from Gatlin O'Donkor and Mo Missanga, lifted them to within touching distance of the top two. Afterwards Ramsey told BBC Sport he was "loving every moment" and was quick to share the credit with his staff.

It has not all been smooth. Oxford went out of the FA Cup to Stevenage (0-1) earlier this month, a reminder that Ramsey's favourite competition does not do sentiment. They bounced back with a 4-0 win over Cheltenham in the EFL Trophy.

What has changed?

Ramsey has not made grand claims about tactics or philosophy in public. What he has talked about, from day one, is standards, professionalism and work ethic, and the results suggest a team that knows exactly what it is doing without the ball: hard to beat, hard to play through and comfortable winning by a goal. His approach on the touchline and in interviews has been plain and direct, with the credit passed to players and staff rather than claimed for himself.

There is something fitting in that. Ramsey was never a self-promoter as a player either. He was the midfielder who did the running others did not see, then popped up with the goal that settled a final. He has done his coaching qualifications the slow way, through the FAW's courses while still playing. He came back from a broken leg, a string of muscle injuries and a deeply personal loss in Mexico. Nothing about his route to the Kassam was easy, and he seems to carry that into the job.

For Oxford supporters who did not want change in June, the evidence of the last five months has done the persuading. A team that was relegated in May has spent the autumn looking like one of the best in the division.

Today: Oxford United v Wycombe Wanderers (15:00, Kassam Stadium)

Of all the fixtures to come next, this one has a twist. Wycombe are the club where Matt Bloomfield, Ramsey's predecessor at Oxford, spent almost his entire career: 558 appearances as a player between 2003 and 2022 and then two years as manager. Ramsey's Oxford now meet them as a promotion side; Wycombe arrive 17th.

Wycombe have their own new man in the dugout. Tom Hounsell took over on 9 September after Michael Duff left (Duff has since taken over at Bradford), and his side arrive unbeaten in five in all competitions, including a 2-0 FA Cup win at Accrington. They have drawn six of their 13 league games, scored 21 and conceded 23, so their games rarely lack goals.

Team news. Oxford are expected to be without Aidomo Emakhu, injured against Cheltenham, with Jin-woo Jeon the likely replacement. Wycombe will miss former England striker Danny Ings with a hamstring problem picked up at Accrington; Jayden Wareham is expected to lead the line. Brannagan, the man whose free kick relegated Ramsey's Cardiff, is in Sports Mole's predicted Oxford midfield.

The head-to-head is the one blot. Oxford have not beaten Wycombe in their last seven meetings in all competitions. If this Oxford side is serious about promotion, this is the kind of record that needs to end.

Can Oxford go straight back up?

In mid-November the promotion market had Oxford at around 3/1 to 10/3 (4.00 to 4.33) to go up, according to the bettingodds.com comparison (prices from 16 November). Those odds imply a chance of roughly 23% to 25%. Huddersfield (4/7) and Stockport (around 1/2) were the market's leading two, with Luton around 11/8 and Sheffield Wednesday around 9/2.

Is 3/1 generous? It is a fair question. Oxford sit fourth, level on points with third, three behind the top two and with games in hand on all three sides above them. Their rate of 2.00 points a game is close to the leaders', and their defence is the second-best in the league. On the other side of the argument: the season is only 13 games old, a squad that has just been relegated has had a big summer of change, and a first-time head coach is learning on the job.

Two-thirds of the season is still to play. In League One, automatic promotion usually takes a points total somewhere in the 80s or more, which means Oxford would need to keep up something close to their current pace until May. Today's game, against a side Oxford have not beaten in seven attempts, is exactly the kind of fixture promotion seasons are built on.

The bigger picture for Oxford

It is easy to forget how much is happening at this club. Oxford United were founded in 1893 as Headington, and their greatest day came on 20 April 1986, when they beat Queens Park Rangers 3-0 at Wembley to win the League (Milk) Cup, during a three-season spell in the old First Division. The ban on English clubs after Heysel denied them a place in the UEFA Cup.

More recently, the club won the 2024 League One play-off final 2-0 against Bolton to return to the Championship after 25 years, then finished 17th in 2024-25 before last season's relegation. Off the pitch, plans for a new 16,000-seat stadium at the Triangle in Kidlington were granted planning permission in August 2025 and given final approval in February 2026; an extension lets Oxford stay at the Kassam until 2028. A club with a new ground on the way and a head coach who has won people over this quickly has every reason to think big.

The bottom line

Aaron Ramsey's career has had everything: a record-breaking teenage debut, a broken leg, two FA Cup final winners, the biggest contract a British player had ever signed, a European Championship semi-final, a penalty saved in a Europa League final, a relegation as a caretaker and a lost dog on the other side of the world. Oxford United took a chance on him in June when they had every reason to play safe.

Thirteen league games later, Oxford have lost once and are a serious promotion side. Today, at home to Wycombe, the market gives them a 40% chance of a win and the head-to-head says they have not managed one in seven attempts. Whatever happens at the Kassam this afternoon, this is already one of the best stories in English football this season.
