Deposit limits, time-outs and GAMSTOP: the tools that keep betting in check, and the new UK rules

Every GB-licensed bookmaker has to offer limits, breaks and self-exclusion. Here is what each tool does, what changed in the rules on 31 October 2025, and what is still to come.

Safer gamblingRegulation
By the BetMeisters.news desk22 November 20266 min read

Betting should stay a hobby with a budget, not something that runs your week. UK rules make every licensed online bookmaker offer the same basic tools to help with that. They are free, they take a minute to set up, and they work best when you set them before you need them.

1. Deposit and other financial limits

A deposit limit caps how much you can pay into your account over a set period, such as a day, a week or a month. Some bookmakers also offer loss limits (how much you can lose) or net limits that take withdrawals into account.

The Gambling Commission's technical standards set two rules that matter most:

  • Lowering a limit takes effect straight away.
  • Raising a limit only happens after a cooling-off period of at least 24 hours, and you then have to confirm it. That pause is deliberate: it stops a limit being lifted in the heat of the moment.

What changed on 31 October 2025

New rules from the Gambling Commission took effect on 31 October 2025. Online operators now have to:

  • prompt you to set a financial limit before your first deposit, with setting a limit as the default (if you choose not to, you have to confirm that);
  • let you type in your own amount rather than pick from a short list;
  • put a clear link to the limit tools on the home page and the deposit pages;
  • remind customers with limits to review their account at least every six months, and prompt active customers without limits to consider one at least every 12 months.

A second stage is due by 30 September 2026 (the Commission extended it from 30 June 2026): every operator must then offer a true gross deposit limit, based only on the money you pay in, and only that kind of limit may be called a "deposit limit".

2. Time-outs: a short break

A time-out (sometimes called "take a break") locks your account for a short spell. Licence conditions require online operators to offer at least 24 hours, one week and one month, or another period you reasonably ask for, up to six weeks. A time-out is useful after a bad run, around a big event, or simply when you want a weekend off. It only covers the one bookmaker you set it with.

3. Self-exclusion with one bookmaker

If you want a longer stop, you can self-exclude with a bookmaker. The Commission's code says the minimum period offered should be between six and 12 months, with the option of at least five years. Once it ends, the exclusion stays in place for up to seven years unless you actively ask to return, which needs a one-day cooling-off and a phone call or visit, not just an online form.

4. GAMSTOP: one block for every GB-licensed site

GAMSTOP is a free scheme that blocks you from online gambling sites and apps licensed in Great Britain in one go, instead of one bookmaker at a time. You choose six months, one year or five years, or five years with automatic renewal. Two things to know before you sign up:

  • You cannot cancel it early: the block runs for the whole period you chose.
  • It does not end automatically: when the period is over, the block stays until you contact GAMSTOP to remove it.

GAMSTOP covers online gambling only. Betting shops, casinos and bingo halls have their own self-exclusion schemes, and blocking software for your phone or laptop and gambling blocks offered by many UK banks can add another layer.

In the US

There is no national scheme like GAMSTOP. Self-exclusion is run state by state, usually by the state's gaming regulator, and each licensed sportsbook also offers its own limits and cool-off tools in the account settings. Help is available on 1-800-GAMBLER.

A simple routine that works

  1. Set a weekly or monthly deposit limit when you open an account, at an amount you would be happy to lose.
  2. Use the same limit at every bookmaker you use.
  3. If you notice yourself chasing losses or betting to feel better, take a time-out first, and think about GAMSTOP if it keeps happening.

Free, confidential help in Great Britain: the National Gambling Helpline on 0808 8020 133, run by GamCare.
