Meister previews · Premier League Gameweek 11

Coventry v Crystal Palace: a relegation six-pointer the market can barely split

Saturday 21 November, 3pm UK. Coventry are 17th on nine points, Palace bottom on seven. The sharp market makes it 38% to 35% with a 27% draw. The table, the form, the goals lines and what the rest of the bottom five face.

Of the ten Premier League games on the first weekend after the international break, none matters more to the bottom of the table than Coventry City v Crystal Palace at the Coventry Building Society Arena, kick-off 3pm UK on Saturday 21 November (10am ET). Coventry are 17th, the last place above the relegation zone. Palace are 20th. A home win would put five points between them; a Palace win would lift Palace above Coventry.

The table

After ten games, the bottom five are separated by two points:

  • 16th Nottingham Forest, 9 points, goal difference -4
  • 17th Coventry City, 9 points, -9
  • 18th Manchester United, 8 points, -5
  • 19th Sunderland, 8 points, -9
  • 20th Crystal Palace, 7 points, -14

Palace's goal difference is the worst in the league, and it is a real handicap in a race this tight: on level points they lose out to everyone around them. Fulham, 15th on ten points, are only one point clear of Coventry.

The form

Coventry have been a strange mix of excellent and poor. Their last six games, most recent first, according to Sports Mole: a 3-1 defeat at Everton, a 3-1 home win over Sunderland, a 1-0 home defeat by Fulham, a 3-1 defeat at Tottenham, a 4-1 home win over Newcastle and a 1-0 win at Nottingham Forest. Three wins, all against teams in or around the bottom half; three defeats, all by two goals or a single goal against sides above them. At home they have beaten Newcastle and Sunderland comfortably.

Palace's run is worse. Their last six in all competitions: a 3-1 home defeat by Liverpool, a 1-1 Europa League draw with Hoffenheim, a 2-0 defeat at Tottenham, a 1-0 League Cup win at Fulham, a 2-0 home defeat by Newcastle and a 2-2 draw at Beşiktaş in Europe. In the league, the run is three defeats in a row, scoring one goal and conceding seven. They are also still in Europe, which means Thursday-to-Sunday weeks that Coventry, without European football, do not have.

The clubs have not met often in recent years. The last meeting Sports Mole lists was a 1-1 draw at Coventry in March 2012.

What the market says

Pinnacle's prices on Wednesday, with the margin removed:

  • Coventry win: about 38.5% (a fair price of about 2.60)
  • Draw: about 26.8% (about 3.73)
  • Palace win: about 34.7% (about 2.88)

That is close to as even as a Premier League game gets. On the Asian handicap the market has Coventry on a level line (draw no bet) at about 52.6%, so it gives the home side a small edge and little more. Since Monday the price has barely moved: on 17 November Coventry were about 39.5% and Palace about 33.8%.

The goals lines point to an open game rather than a nervy one. Over 2.5 goals is about 56%, close to the average for this weekend's fixtures. Each side is close to a coin flip to score twice: Coventry about 45% and Palace about 42% to score two or more. Put together, the market's most likely shapes are a narrow win either way or a scoring draw, not a goalless game.

Why the draw is not a 'safe' result for either side

The draw is priced at about 27%, but for Palace it would leave them bottom, two points behind Coventry, with the worst goal difference in the division. For Coventry, it would keep them two points clear of Palace but would do nothing about the clubs just below them. That changes how a game like this is played late on: whichever side is behind after 70 minutes has more reason to chase, which is one reason the market keeps the goals lines high.

The rest of the bottom five this weekend

The results around this game matter almost as much, and on paper they are hard for everyone involved:

  • Bournemouth v Nottingham Forest (Saturday, 3pm): Forest about 24%, Bournemouth about 50%.
  • Aston Villa v Sunderland (Saturday, 3pm): Sunderland about 22%, Villa about 52%.
  • Liverpool v Manchester United (Sunday, 4.30pm): United about 23%, Liverpool about 55%.

All three of the clubs around Coventry and Palace are underdogs away from home. That makes Saturday's game at Coventry the most likely source of points for anyone in the bottom five, and a winner here could climb out of the bottom three or put real distance between themselves and it in a single afternoon.

For bettors

A market this even is a market where a small price difference between bookmakers is worth looking for. At fair prices of about 2.60 and 2.88, any firm offering 2.75 or more on Coventry, or 3.05 or more on Palace, would be offering value against the sharp line, as would anything above about 1.90 on Coventry draw no bet. Those numbers will move with team news on Thursday and Friday. If there is a BetMeisters tip on this game it will go onto BetMeisters.com with its price, our chance and the edge.

The verdict from the numbers

Coventry about 38.5%, Palace about 34.7%, the draw about 26.8%. The table says Palace are in trouble; the market says they are almost as likely as Coventry to win this one. Coventry's home form against teams around them has been good, Palace's league run is three defeats in a row, and yet the gap in the price is small. Watch the team news: in a game this tight, one absence can move the market more than the table does.
