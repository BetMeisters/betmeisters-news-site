Wednesday's football: Chelsea v PSG in the Women's Champions League, Copa del Rey ties and a Brasileirão night

The internationals are over, so Wednesday is a club night: the Women's Champions League, the Women's League Cup, six Copa del Rey ties and six Brazilian Série A games. Every main game with its chance on the sharp market.

FootballSports betting
By the BetMeisters.news desk17 November 20267 min read

After a full week of internationals, Wednesday is quieter, and almost all club football. The headline games in Europe are in the UEFA Women's Champions League, with Chelsea at home to Paris Saint-Germain and Manchester City away in Sweden. England's Women's League Cup has four group games, Spain's Copa del Rey has six ties, and in the evening the Brasileirão has six games, including Flamengo and Palmeiras.

The chances below come from Pinnacle, the sharpest bookmaker in the market, with its margin removed. They show what the market thinks, not what we think. A chance is not a prediction: a 60% favourite fails to win four times in ten. All times are UK time, with US Eastern time for the evening games.

Updated 07:37 Kampala: the morning market

BetMeisters' 7.30am price snapshot (Pinnacle, margin removed) has moved the big Women's Champions League games a little. Chelsea are now about 76% to beat PSG (72.3% on Tuesday), with PSG about 9% and over 2.5 goals about 66%. Juventus have eased slightly to about 74% against OH Leuven (77.0%). Manchester City are about 76.5% in Gothenburg, much as they were.

Elsewhere the numbers have barely changed: West Ham about 84% against Wolves in the Women's League Cup, Liverpool about 67% at Nottingham Forest, Mallorca about 61% at Alcoyano, Flamengo about 77.5% against Athletico Paranaense and Cruzeiro v Palmeiras still close to a three-way split (35% / 27% / 37%).

We have not added team news: none of it had been confirmed by a source we trust at the time of writing.

Women's Champions League

Chelsea v Paris Saint-Germain · 8.00pm (3.00pm ET)

The biggest game of the night in England. The market makes Chelsea 72.3%, the draw 17.1% and PSG 10.6%, a clear home favourite. It also expects goals: over 2.5 goals is about 61%.

Häcken v Manchester City · 5.45pm (12.45pm ET)

City travel to Gothenburg as heavy favourites: Manchester City 74.5%, the draw 15.7%, Häcken 9.9%. Over 2.5 goals is about 65%.

Juventus v OH Leuven · 8.00pm (3.00pm ET)

The most one-sided game of the three: Juventus 77.0%, Leuven 8.1%, and over 2.5 goals about 66%, the highest of the European games.

Women's League Cup · 7.00pm

Four group games kick off at the same time. West Ham v Wolves is the most lopsided: West Ham about 84%. Nottingham Forest v Liverpool has Liverpool about 67% away. Manchester United v Everton has United about 64%, and London City Lionesses v Brighton has London City about 58%. The market expects goals in all of them: over 2.5 is between 61% and 64% in the three games where it is priced. Prices on the women's game are thinner than on the men's, so they can move a lot once team news is out.

And the rest

Earlier in the day, the ASEAN Club Championship has Buriram United about 45.5% away to Cong An Ho Chi Minh City (11.00am). Overnight into Wednesday (3.00am UK, 10.00pm ET on Tuesday), Nicaragua are 79% favourites against Montserrat in the CONCACAF Nations League play-offs.

The favourites of the night

Put together, Wednesday has an unusual number of very short favourites. On the sharp market, six sides are 70% or more to win in 90 minutes:

  • West Ham v Wolves (Women's League Cup): about 84%
  • Flamengo v Athletico Paranaense: 78.0%
  • Juventus v OH Leuven: 77.0%
  • Manchester City at Häcken: 74.5%
  • Chelsea v PSG: 72.3%
  • Nicaragua v Montserrat: 79.3%

Short favourites are the most popular legs in accumulators, and it is worth being clear about what they mean. Even if each of those six really is as likely to win as the market says, the chance that all six win is about 0.84 × 0.78 × 0.77 × 0.745 × 0.723 × 0.793, or roughly 21%, about one in five. Each leg you add multiplies in another chance of something going wrong, which is why a six-fold of 'bankers' still loses about four times in five.

Two kinds of goals market

The most striking pattern on Wednesday's card is the gap between the women's games and the Spanish cup ties. In every women's game where it is priced, over 2.5 goals is 61% or higher: 61% in Chelsea v PSG and London City v Brighton, 64% in Nottingham Forest v Liverpool, 65% in Häcken v Manchester City and 66% in Juventus v Leuven. In the Copa del Rey it is below 50% in every tie where it is priced: 47% to 48% in Alcoyano v Mallorca, Badajoz v Tenerife and Utebo v Sabadell, 44% in Portugalete v Real Unión and only 40% in Minera v Cartagena.

The reasons are not mysterious. The women's games involve bigger gaps in quality between the sides, and big gaps produce goals at one end. Early-round cup ties between a lower-division home side and a stronger visitor tend to be cagey for an hour: the home team defends deep, the visitors are often rotated, and a single goal can decide it. When you see a goals line, it is worth asking which kind of game you are looking at before you look at the price.

Cup ties: what '90 minutes' means

Copa del Rey ties are settled on the night, so a level score after 90 minutes goes to extra time and, if needed, penalties. That matters for betting. Almost every match-result market (home, draw, away) and every goals line is settled on the 90 minutes only: a draw bet wins if it is level at 90 minutes, even if one side goes through later. There is usually a separate market, 'to qualify' or 'to progress', that includes extra time and penalties, and its prices are different.

On Wednesday's ties the market puts the 90-minute draw at between 24% and 31%. In Minera v Cartagena and Portugalete v Real Unión it is close to 30%, so roughly three ties in ten like those go beyond 90 minutes. If you back a favourite 'to win' in a cup tie, you are not backing it to go through; you are backing it to win inside 90 minutes.

Brazil in more detail

The Brasileirão games run through the US evening, and the market has a clear view of each one. Cruzeiro v Palmeiras is the tightest: no outcome is above 39%, and over 2.5 goals is about 54%. Corinthians v Botafogo is a coin flip on goals (over 2.5 about 51%), while Grêmio v Bahia leans to goals at about 57%. Remo v Chapecoense has the home side 61% with over 2.5 goals at 61%, and Coritiba v Santos has the visitors as favourites at 46%, with over 2.5 goals about 55%.

Home advantage in Brazil is often said to be strong, with long journeys across a big country and very different conditions from city to city. Whatever its size, the market already includes it, which is why Flamengo are such short favourites at home and why Santos are only 46% away even as favourites.
